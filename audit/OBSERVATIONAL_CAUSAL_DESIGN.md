# OBSERVATIONAL CAUSAL DESIGN

## Goal
To demonstrate why simple machine learning models (Naive Propensity) fail when trained on historical data generated by a biased policy, and why causal models (T-Learner, DR-Learner) are necessary to untangle treatment effects from confounding variables.

## Treatment Assignment Bias (Confounding)
In the observational regime, the probability of receiving a specific action depends on observable features ($X$) that ALSO predict the natural recovery rate.

We implemented the following historical policy logits:
1. `RETRY_NOW`: heavily biased towards low-value customers (`amount < 100k`).
2. `RETRY_LATER`: heavily biased towards `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`.
3. `PAYMENT_MESSAGE`: heavily biased towards highly engaged customers (`engagement > 0.7`).
4. `ALTERNATE_METHOD`: uniform baseline.
5. `HUMAN_ESCALATION`: heavily biased towards VIPs (`value_band == 'high'`) and explicitly withheld from low-value bands.

## Why Naive Models Fail
Because VIPs and highly engaged customers have high natural recovery rates, actions like `HUMAN_ESCALATION` and `PAYMENT_MESSAGE` will have high empirical success rates. 

A Naive Recovery Propensity model fits a single Logistic Regression predicting $Y$ from $(X, A)$. Because it lacks full interaction terms ($X \times A$), it absorbs the baseline success of VIPs into the coefficient for $A=5$ (Human). It mistakenly concludes that Human Escalation *caused* the recovery, when in reality the customer was a VIP who was likely to recover anyway.

## Causal Estimation Advantage
The T-Learner fits separate models for each action branch ($E[Y | X, A=a]$ and $E[Y | X, A=0]$). When it evaluates the treatment effect $E[Y|a] - E[Y|0]$ for a VIP, it correctly recognizes that $E[Y|0]$ (doing nothing) is *already* very high. Therefore, the subtraction cancels out the confounding bias, revealing that the true incremental uplift of Human Escalation is small.

## Doubly Robust (DR) Learner Expected Behavior
The Observational Regime is where the DR-Learner is designed to shine. By weighting the pseudo-outcomes with Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW), it actively debiases the training samples. Cases that received a treatment contrary to historical bias (e.g., a low-value customer receiving Human Escalation) are up-weighted, providing cleaner estimates of the true heterogeneous treatment effect than standard regression.
